Sandchest has one plan: $1 a month, and credits bought ahead at 100 for $1, less when you buy more at once. Pull request reviews and deep scans are charged for what they use, and you see the most a scan can cost before it starts. There are no per-seat prices and no repository limits: credits are the only limit.

## Plans

| Plan | Per month | Credits | Price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | $1 | Bought as you need them | $1 per 100, down to $0.80 per 100 in bulk |

- Credits are bought ahead and never expire. Buy them when you join, from **Settings → Billing** at any time, or let auto top-up buy them when you run low.
- The customer is the GitHub account Sandchest is installed on, not a person. Everyone with access to that account's repositories shares its credits.
- Prices are in US dollars. Stripe may show the amount in your local currency at checkout.
- Balances were multiplied by 100 when a credit changed from $1 to 1 cent, so nobody's balance lost value.

### Earlier plans

Accounts on an earlier plan keep it, at the same price, until they switch. Its monthly credits are 100 times what they were.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Lockbox | $20 | 6,000 |
| Chest | $100 | 35,000 |
| Vault | $250 | 100,000 |

## What scans cost

| Scan | When it runs | Credits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recon | When a repository is added | Free |
| PR review | A pull request is opened or updated | What it uses |
| Deep scan | When you press **Deep scan** | What it uses |
| Fix check | Pushes to the default branch | Free |

Recon and fix checks are free. Both need the account to be on a plan. Recon also needs at least one credit left: with none, it waits until the account buys more and then starts on its own. Fix checks run even with no credits left, so a bug you fixed is never left open waiting on a top-up.

### Charged for what it uses

A pull request review or a deep scan is charged for the work it does: the model's reading and reasoning, and the time it spends in a sandbox.

- Before a scan starts, Sandchest shows the most it can cost, as "up to" a number of credits, and holds that amount.
- When the scan finishes, it is charged what it used and the rest of the hold goes back to your balance. It is never charged more than the amount shown.
- A scan that fails or is refused is not charged, and the whole hold goes back.

### What to expect

These are typical amounts, not promises. The exact maximum for each scan is shown before it starts.

- Most pull request reviews cost a few credits or less. A pull request that changes nothing security-relevant, such as one that only touches docs, tests, lockfiles or generated files, costs nothing.
- A deep scan usually costs well under 100 credits.
- Large repositories cost more, because there is more code to read.

### The bill

Every charged scan has an itemised bill on its page: what it spent hunting, scoring, verifying in a sandbox, looking for variants and writing up, and its sandbox time, each in credits. **Settings → Billing** lists recent usage across the account.

Charges are in whole credits. A fraction of a credit left over is carried to the account's next scan, so nothing is rounded away in either direction.

### Fast scans

Where the option is shown, a deep scan can be started as **Fast**. It uses a faster model and costs about 3x as much on average. Its maximum is shown before it starts, like any other scan.

### How long scans take

A deep scan of a large repository typically takes 15 to 30 minutes, because every candidate is verified in a sandbox before it is reported. You can follow it live on the scan's page.

### Large repositories

Repositories of a million lines or more aren't scanned at all, by recon, reviews or deep scans. Get in touch if you need one scanned.

## When credits run out

- A pull request that arrives when the account is out of credits isn't reviewed. Its Check is **neutral** ("Out of credits"), so it never blocks a merge, and it links to where you can top up. The same happens, with "Not reviewed: no plan", before the account has a plan. Later pushes to that pull request aren't reviewed either, and get no new Check, until the account can pay again; the next push after that is reviewed.
- When there aren't enough credits to cover a deep scan's maximum, the repository shows how many it needs, with a link to top up, instead of the **Deep scan** button.
- With auto top-up off, the account's admins get an email when credits run low (below 500) and when they run out. With it on, they get an email for each top-up, and one if a top-up fails.

## Free credits

Testers can claim 2,000 free credits from [sandchest.com/claim](/claim), or by typing a tester's code into **Have a code?** at checkout, with no card. They work like bought credits: reviews and deep scans both draw on them.

- One claim per GitHub account, and one per person, for an account that has never had a plan. Only an admin of the account can claim.
- The account is on **Free credits** until it joins Standard. Nothing can be charged on it: there are no top-ups or auto top-up.
- When the credits are spent, every paid scan and recon stops (pull requests get the neutral "Out of credits" Check) until the account joins Standard from **Settings → Billing**, which takes a card on Stripe. Any free credits left over carry across.

## Buying credits

Credits are 100 for $1 and never expire. They come in packs of 100, so the smallest purchase is 100 credits. Buy 1,000, 2,500, 5,000 or 10,000 when you join, and up to 100,000 at once after that. Buying more at once costs less: 2,500 or more are $0.90 per 100, 5,000 or more $0.85, and 10,000 or more $0.80. The whole purchase is priced at the rate its size reaches, and auto top-ups get the same discount.

| Credits bought at once | Per 100 credits | For example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 to 2,499 | $1.00 | 1,000 for $10 |
| 2,500 to 4,999 | $0.90 | 2,500 for $22.50, saving $2.50 |
| 5,000 to 9,999 | $0.85 | 5,000 for $42.50, saving $7.50 |
| 10,000 or more | $0.80 | 10,000 for $80, saving $20 |

### Auto top-up

Auto top-up buys credits for you when fewer than 500 are left, so pull requests keep getting reviewed. You choose how many it buys each time (the same amount you started with, unless you change it). It spends at most $500 a month unless you change that in **Settings → Billing**, where you can raise the limit, lower it or remove it. Once it has spent the limit in a month, it stops until the next, and the account's admins get an email. It's on when you join unless you turn it off, and you can change or turn it off in Settings. Auto top-up charges your card directly, without a confirm page.

## Managing billing

Everything lives in [Settings → Billing](/dashboard/settings/billing). Only an admin of the GitHub organization, or the owner of a personal account, can change it.

- **Switching from an earlier plan.** Accounts on Lockbox, Chest or Vault can switch to Standard from Settings. Credits already bought stay.
- **Confirm before charging.** When a card is already on file, every credit purchase and plan change is shown on a confirm page, with the exact amount, before it is made. Auto top-up is the exception: it charges on its own, within any monthly limit you set.
- **Business invoices.** When you join, you can add your business's name, billing address and tax ID (a VAT number, EIN, ABN or the like, by country). Stripe keeps them and puts them on every invoice; Sandchest doesn't store them.
- **Card, invoices and cancelling** are handled in Stripe's customer portal, linked from Settings. A cancelled plan keeps working until the end of the period you paid for.

Payments are processed by Stripe, through the billing service Autumn. Sandchest never sees your card number.

## Self-hosted

A self-hosted Sandchest has billing turned off: no plan, no credits and nothing to buy. You pay only for your own servers and model usage. The operational limits still apply: one deep scan at a time per repository, no scans of repositories with a million lines or more, and at most two scans at a time per account, with two more for recon. See [Self-hosting](/docs/self-hosting#limits-without-billing).
