Sandchest is run by {legal.entity} ("Cap", "we", "us"), of {legal.address}. This policy explains how we handle information when you use the hosted Sandchest service. We are the controller of the personal information described here.

The short version: **we never store your source code.** Every scan runs in a temporary environment that's deleted when the scan finishes. Model inference runs with zero data retention. We keep only your findings and the minimum code excerpts needed to explain them. We don't sell personal information.

A self-hosted Sandchest sends nothing to us, so this policy doesn't apply to it.

## What we collect

**When you sign in with GitHub**: your GitHub user ID, username, display name, avatar and primary email address, and a sign-in token, which we store encrypted. We get your primary email even if it's private on GitHub, because the App has permission to read email addresses. If GitHub returns none, you get no email from us. Each session also stores your browser's user agent.

**When you install the App**: the account it's installed on, the repositories you chose (names, whether they're private, default branches), who the account's admins are, and which repositories each signed-in person can push to.

**From scans**:

- findings, each with short excerpts of the code along its attack path
- each repository's threat model: a structured summary of its entry points, trust boundaries and risky code, not the code itself
- scan records: what ran, when, how long it took, what it found and what it cost, and its progress messages

**From billing**: your plan, credit balance and auto top-up settings. Payments are made to Stripe through our billing provider, Autumn. We never see or store your card number.

**From using the site and the dashboard**: first-party usage events, which we store ourselves. They record the pages you view, with any IDs removed from the address, and how long each was on screen. They also record the links and tracked buttons you click, the steps of signing up you reach, and actions such as starting a scan, buying credits or copying a fix prompt, with whether each one went through. A page view also records the site that sent you (its domain only), any campaign tags in the link, your device type (phone, tablet or desktop) and your country. Events are tied to a random ID kept in a cookie, and to your account once you sign in, so we can see how people find and use Sandchest. They hold opaque IDs, counts and categories only: never code, finding contents, repository names, names, email addresses or IP addresses.

**From agents you connect**: the name the agent or you gave it, when it was last used, and a hash of its key or token (never the key itself). Changes an agent makes through the API are recorded as usage events like the ones above: which action, whether it went through, and whether it came over MCP or HTTP.

**Web analytics from our hosting provider**: page view counts and page-speed measurements, collected without cookies and without storing IP addresses.

## What we don't collect

- **Your source code.** Each scan clones the one commit it needs into a temporary workspace, and destroys the workspace when the scan ends, whether it succeeded or failed. We keep no copies, snapshots or diffs.
- **IP addresses** with your account. Sign-in doesn't record them.
- **Advertising tracking.** There are no advertising scripts or tracking pixels, and nothing is shared with advertisers. Our cookies keep you signed in, hold the random ID for our own usage events (for a year), and, if you came to claim free credits, remember that while you sign up.

## How code is processed

During a scan, code is read in the temporary workspace and sent to the model that analyses it. Our policy is to use model inference with zero data retention: the provider we use is set up not to keep prompts or outputs.

Sandchest adds no account, repository, organization or user identifiers to prompts. But your code goes to the model as it is, so anything written in it does too, such as names, email addresses or URLs in comments.

We don't use your code, findings or prompts to train models.

## How we use information

- to run scans, show you their results, and post reviews and Checks on your pull requests
- to decide who can see which findings, using GitHub's own permissions
- to bill your plan
- to email people with access about new critical findings and finished deep scans (an admin can turn these off in Settings)
- to email the account's admins about billing: credits running low or out, auto top-ups, and scans stopped by the model provider. Billing emails can't be turned off; the scan-stopped email follows the account's email setting
- to keep the service secure and working, and to improve it from aggregate usage
- to meet our legal obligations

If you're in the EU or UK, our legal bases are: performing our contract with you, our legitimate interest in running a secure and working service, and complying with the law.

We don't sell or share personal information for advertising, and we don't send marketing email.

## Public repositories

Checks and review comments on public repositories are visible to everyone. For public repositories Sandchest posts only a count of findings by severity, and keeps the details in the dashboard.

## Who we share it with

We use a small number of service providers (subprocessors) to host the service, run model inference, send email and process payments. They act on our instructions and only to provide Sandchest. The current list is available on request at <Email to={legal.email} />.

We disclose information to authorities only when the law requires it, and to a buyer or successor if Cap is involved in a merger, acquisition or sale of assets, under this policy.

We're based in the United States, and information is processed there and wherever our providers operate. Where the law requires it, we protect transfers with safeguards such as the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

## How long we keep it

- **Code excerpts** are deleted, along with the fix prompt that quoted them, as soon as their finding is fixed or dismissed, when its repository is removed from the installation, or when the App is uninstalled or suspended.
- **Findings, threat models and scan records** are kept so you keep your history, including after you uninstall, when findings are kept without code. Write to us to have them deleted.
- **If you uninstall** the App, no new scans start and we lose access to your repositories. A scan already running may still finish.
- **Your account** is kept until you ask us to delete it.
- **Billing records** are kept as long as tax and accounting law requires.

## Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or delete your personal information, and to object to or restrict how we use it. Write to <Email to={legal.email} /> and we'll respond within 30 days. We won't treat you differently for using these rights. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Sandchest isn't for children, and we don't knowingly collect information from anyone under 16.

## Security

Access to findings follows GitHub: a person sees a repository's findings only if they have push access to it. GitHub tokens are short-lived; the one used to clone can only read the contents of the repository being scanned. Sign-in tokens are encrypted at rest. More detail is in [Privacy & security](/docs/security).
